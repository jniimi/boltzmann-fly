# Step 1: masked Boltzmann machine and the ablation ladder (design note)

Text only. Companion to `docs/step0_mb_stats.md`. Results live in `docs/results/` (`results.csv`,
`summary.md`, one JSON per run under `runs/`).

## 1. What is reproduced

The ICONIP 2026 Purchase-World experiment, using the configuration of that experiment as recovered
from the original run's logged metadata: 1,024 consumers x 365 days, 10 stores, lag window 4, data
seed 42; validation and test = the last 30 + 30 days; DBM hidden layers 64-32-16; greedy pretraining
100 epochs per layer; joint fine-tuning up to 300 epochs; adapters 64-32-16 for up to 100 epochs.
Training details: DBM batch 128; greedy pretraining Adam lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-3, PCD k=1; joint
fine-tuning Adam lr 1e-5, weight decay 1e-4, PCD k=5, 10 mean-field iterations, patience 20 on
validation reconstruction BCE (the original run used all 300 epochs; best epoch restored); adapters
and baseline MLP = MLP 64-32-16, ReLU, dropout 0.1, Adam lr 5e-4, batch 256, patience 30 on
validation AUC, threshold chosen on validation. The split is by *time* (days 0-304 / 305-334 /
335-364; every consumer appears in every split), which gives 312,320 / 30,720 / 30,720 rows.

The simulation is generated by the vendored generator with the numpy legacy RNG, so the panel is
bit-identical to the original (`tests/test_vendor_and_data.py` checks it against stored digests). It
is cached under `/Volumes/EXTERNAL/malecns/derived/boltzmann-fly/data/pw_n1024_d365_s10_ws4_seed42_v30_t30/`.

The three tasks follow the paper and the original code paths (vendored; see `src/boltzmann_fly/vendor/NOTICE`):

1. **Energy consistency** (paper Sec. 4.1): eligible rows have
   `purchase_lag1..4 = 0`; clamp `purchase_lag1..4 = 1`, `purchased_within_7d = 1`,
   `campaign_lag1..4 = push_lag1..4 = 0`; report the mean of dF = F(clamped) - F(original), paired t,
   Wilcoxon, and the median split on true beta (Welch t, Mann-Whitney). As a scalar summary we also
   report AUC(F separates clamped from original vectors) and the fraction of dF > 0. For the fly
   variants the clamp is applied to the 72 features and then embedded into the PN layer.
2. **Prediction**: visit / purchase adapters on the frozen belief (all hidden layers concatenated,
   "full", and top layer only, "top") versus the baseline MLP on the raw 72 features + action.
3. **Counterfactual uplift**: belief fixed, `push` (visit) or `sale1` (purchase) toggled; Spearman rho
   between the logit-scale uplift and true gamma / alpha / beta on the test split. The adapter used
   is the one with the better validation AUC (full or top), as in the original.

## 3. Controls

- **degree**: degree-preserving random rewiring. For each bipartite 0/1 mask separately, Maslov-Sneppen
  swaps pick two edges (a,b), (c,d) with a != c, b != d and (a,d), (c,b) absent, and replace them by
  (a,d), (c,b); this preserves every row degree (per PN fan-out) and every column degree (per KC
  fan-in, per MBON fan-in). We perform 10 x E successful swaps. The overlap with the real mask drops
  to about 9 % (PN-KC) and 38 % (KC-MBON; that matrix is dense enough that random swaps keep many
  edges). The feature -> unit assignment and the visible layer are unchanged.
- **er**: Erdos-Renyi bipartite graph of the same shape and edge count (overlap = density).

Both controls keep the number of trainable couplings, the layer sizes and therefore the belief
dimension identical to the real wiring.

## 4. Masked DBM

`src/boltzmann_fly/masked_dbm.py` subclasses the vendored `RestrictedBoltzmannMachine` and
`DeepBoltzmannMachine`. Each coupling matrix has a fixed 0/1 buffer; (1) every forward use goes
through `effective_weight() = W * mask` (with Dale: `|W| * sign * mask`), (2) a gradient hook
multiplies the gradient by the mask, (3) the raw parameter is re-masked in place after every
`train_step`, after `initialize_from_rbms` and after `load_state_dict`, so checkpoints contain
exact zeros. The vendored training loops (`greedy_pretrain_dbm`, `joint_finetuning`) are unchanged:
`masked_classes(...)` swaps the class names they look up so they build masked objects with the
mask whose shape matches each layer. `tests/test_masked_dbm.py` checks that the all-ones-mask model is
numerically identical to the upstream classes (inference, free energy, and five training steps with
identical RNG), that masked entries are exactly zero after pretraining + fine-tuning + pickling, and
the Dale signs.

V0 runs through the unmodified vendored classes (no monkeypatch); `--force-masked` runs V0 through
`MaskedDBM` with all-ones masks for verification.

The adapters and the baseline MLP keep the original architecture and hyperparameters regardless of
the belief dimension (1,941 for the fly variants versus 112).

V0 reproduces the paper within seed noise (adapter within 0.002 AUC of the MLP; same signs and
magnitudes of the CATE correlations; the clamp penalty positive and smaller for high-beta consumers).

**Why V1 / V2 fall below the raw-feature baseline.** The belief, not the adapter, is the problem:
a logistic regression on the frozen belief gives the same AUC as the MLP adapter (V1 0.656 / 0.594),
and the adapters underfit (train AUC 0.66). After training, the KC layer is almost constant across
samples: median per-unit standard deviation 0.008 (V1) and 0.003 (V2) versus 0.30 in V0; 59 % (V1)
and 85 % (V2) of KCs are "low variance", and the MBON layer has median sd 0.000. The cause is the
fan-in: a KC receives a median of 5 PN inputs in V1 (2 in V2), of which only the data-carrying ones
vary, and with the ICONIP learning rates (1e-4 pretraining, 1e-5 fine-tuning, weight decay 1e-3)
the learned couplings stay at |w| ~ 0.2, so the total drive per KC (sum of |w| over its fan-in) has
median 0.67 in V1 and 0.02 in V2 versus 22.6 in V0. The visible information is therefore not lost
at the input (every feature is carried by >= 1 PN and reaches ~57 KCs in V1) but is not amplified
into the hidden layers; the 49-unit top layer is not the bottleneck (the "full" belief, which
includes all 1,892 KCs, is equally uninformative). This is a hyperparameter / scale mismatch between
a dense fan-in of 72 and a sparse fan-in of 2-5, not a property of the wiring pattern itself; the
degree-preserving and ER controls have the same fan-in distribution and are expected to behave the
same way under the ICONIP settings.

**The higher energy-test AUC / frac(dF > 0) of V1 / V2 is not a real consistency signal.** Decomposing
dF into the visible-bias part -(v_clamped - v_orig) . b_v and the coupling part: in V1 the correlation
between dF and the bias-only part is 0.995 (V2 0.914; V0 0.426), i.e. the sparse models penalise the
clamp by the marginal log-odds of the clamped units almost independently of the rest of the vector.
That gives a near-deterministic positive dF (high frac_pos / AUC) but no dependence on latent
heterogeneity (Welch t ~ +1 / +3.7 with the wrong sign). The larger mean in V1 (+5.5) is because each
clamped feature is copied to two sister PNs, doubling the bias penalty (bias-only mean 2.35 in all
three models). So it is a scale artefact of the visible encoding plus the weak couplings, not
evidence of better consistency scoring.

## 8. Stage A: fan-in scale fix (non-degeneracy check only; not performance tuning)

Goal: check whether the flat KC layer of Section 7 is merely an initialisation / step-size scale
problem. Two options were added for the fly variants only (V0 always uses the ICONIP settings):

- `--init fanin`: W_ij ~ N(0, sigma^2 / fan_in_j) with sigma = 1, where fan_in_j is the number of
  unmasked inputs of hidden unit j. With binary inputs the initial drive sum_i v_i W_ij then has
  variance sigma^2 x (active inputs / fan_in_j) <= 1, i.e. O(1) for every unit; upstream uses
  N(0, 0.01^2) per entry, which gives a drive of about 0.01 sqrt(fan_in).
- `--lr-scale X`: multiplies both Adam learning rates (pretraining 1e-4, fine-tuning 1e-5). Weight
  decay is left unchanged: torch Adam adds wd*w to the gradient before normalisation, so the
  shrinkage per step already scales with the learning rate.

10 epochs; rows tagged `diagA` in `docs/results/results.csv`, excluded from the main table; test split):

Criterion: median KC belief sd >= ~0.1 (V0: 0.17 on the same test-split measure). None of the
three settings reaches it; the KC layer is even flatter with larger learning rates, and the KC
drive stays at 0.4-0.9 although the fan-in initialisation starts it at about 1.8 (the couplings
shrink during training). The adapter AUCs and CATE correlations are indistinguishable from the
ICONIP-setting V1 run. Conclusion: fan-in-scaled initialisation and learning-rate scaling do not
revive the KC layer; per the project scope the tuning stops here and the Stage B control runs use
the unchanged ICONIP settings, so that all fly variants are comparable. V1 below V0 on every task
is the expected outcome of this project ("it runs"), not a result to be closed.

### Mechanism: maximum likelihood has no pressure to grow couplings onto nearly independent inputs

The couplings of a Boltzmann machine are driven by the difference between data and model
statistics <v_i h_j>; a hidden unit can lower the free energy of the data only by capturing
dependence *among* its inputs, because the marginals of single visible units are already modelled
by the visible biases. In V0 every hidden unit sees all 72 features, which include strongly
dependent one-hot groups (mean pairwise mutual information within the age and income groups 0.05
nats, store 0.011, day-of-week 0.024; maximum pairwise MI 0.30 nats), so the gradient bootstraps
large couplings (median drive 22.6). A V1 KC sees the data-carrying inputs of 2-5 PNs chosen at
random with respect to the features, and those features are almost always independent: over the
72 features the median pairwise MI is 0.0000 nats (mean 0.0035), and the median total pairwise MI
among a KC's inputs is 0.0000. Checked against the trained checkpoints (test split, KC belief sd
and the summed |w| from data-carrying inputs per KC):

| run | KCs with 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+ data inputs | KC sd median by n_in (0 / 1 / 2 / 3+) | rho(sd, n_in) | rho(data drive, total MI of inputs), KCs with >= 2 inputs | data-drive median, inputs sharing > 0.01 nats vs independent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 (ICONIP) | 287 / 622 / 582 / 401 | 0.002 / 0.004 / 0.008 / 0.015 | +0.63 | +0.44 (n = 983) | 0.70 vs 0.16 |
| V1 fanin, lr x10 (diag) | 287 / 622 / 582 / 401 | 0.003 / 0.008 / 0.021 / 0.039 | +0.64 | +0.45 | 1.68 vs 0.42 |
| V1b (ICONIP) | 302 / 596 / 594 / 400 | 0.000 / 0.001 / 0.001 / 0.003 | +0.65 | +0.46 (n = 994) | 0.23 vs 0.04 |
| V2 (ICONIP) | 287 / 622 / 582 / 401 | 0.000 / 0.001 / 0.002 / 0.005 | +0.62 | +0.51 (n = 983) | 1.16 vs 0.04 |

So (i) 287 KCs receive no data at all (their PN inputs belong to unchosen types) and are constant
by construction; (ii) 622 KCs receive exactly one feature, for which no coupling can improve the
likelihood, and they stay flat; (iii) among KCs with two or more inputs, the learned coupling
strength tracks the mutual information shared by those inputs (Spearman +0.44 to +0.51 in all
four checkpoints; 4-30x larger drive when the inputs share > 0.01 nats), and only 6 % of KCs (V1)
reach sd >= 0.1. The hypothesis is therefore supported: a random feature-to-PN assignment gives
each KC an input set with essentially no internal dependence, so the couplings are not grown, the
KC layer stays near its bias-determined activity, and the belief is nearly flat regardless of
initialisation or step size. This is a property of the (assignment x wiring) combination under the
ICONIP training objective, not of the learning rate; a non-random assignment that places
dependent features on PNs converging onto the same KCs would be the way to test the wiring itself,
and is outside the present scope.

### Dissection of ER versus degree-preserving (mask files and checkpoints)

1. **Input coverage is the primary difference.** At w >= 5 only 136 of the 343 right-hemisphere PNs
   have any KC edge (68 of the 177 PN types); the other 207 PNs (104 GABAergic, 99 cholinergic, 4
   glutamatergic) contact KCs only below threshold or not at all. The random type selection of V1 /
   V2 (seed 0) picked 52 types without KC output, so **only 20 of the 72 Purchase-World features
   reach any KC** in V1, V2 and both degree-preserving controls (degree preservation keeps every
   zero out-degree): 14 of the 40 static one-hot features, 3 of the 12 state features and 3 of the
   20 lag features. V1b (72 random neurons) connects 30 of 72. The ER control assigns every PN
   12-48 KC targets (median 28), so **all 72 features enter the model**, each reaching a median of
   41 KCs. This alone explains ER = baseline on prediction and why the fly wiring and its
   degree-preserving control coincide: the 52 missing features, including almost all lagged
   actions and outcomes, are exactly what the visit / purchase adapters and the counterfactual
   contrasts need. It is a property of the feature-to-type assignment (random over all 177 types,
   as specified), not of the mushroom-body wiring among the PNs that do project to KCs; the
   `--type-select top-fanout` option would have avoided it and is the obvious next mask to run.
2. **Degrees.** PN out-degree: real / degree-preserving min 0, median 0, q75 45, max 437 (207 zeros; 91
   of the 142 data-carrying PNs are zeros); ER min 12, median 28, max 48, no zeros. KC in-degree is
   the same in all three (median 5, max 15-17). Distinct data features per KC (0 / 1 / 2 / 3+):
   real 287 / 622 / 582 / 401, degree-preserving 285 / 649 / 517 / 441, ER 240 / 474 / 516 / 662.
3. **KC -> MBON was re-randomised as well.** The ER control rewires both bipartite masks. Real and
   degree-preserving MBON in-degree: min 0, q25 7, median 106, q75 505, max 1,374 (9 MBONs with no
   KC input); ER: min 224, median 254, max 308. In ER the MBON layer saturates (mean activation
   0.988, 47 of 49 units with mean > 0.99, MBON drive 44 vs 12) and carries nothing (logistic
   readout from the 49 MBONs: visit 0.633 / purchase 0.568, the same as in the other two runs).
4. **Which layer carries the information in ER: the KC layer.** Logistic readout from the 1,892 KCs
   alone gives visit 0.711 / purchase 0.675 (real wiring 0.655 / 0.594; degree-preserving 0.655 /
   0.594); adding the MBONs changes nothing. The KC layer of ER relays the raw features almost
   linearly: 49 of the 60 non-month features are recoverable from it with R^2 > 0.9 (real 15, degree
   15; those 15 are the connected one-hot features), and 188 KCs are lively (sd >= 0.1) versus 117 /
   116. The median KC is still flat in ER (sd 0.007), i.e. ER did not "revive" the layer; it added
   input coverage, and the lively minority of KCs that receive dependent inputs copies the features.
5. **The mutual-information mechanism of Section 8 holds in all three checkpoints**, and most
   strongly in ER: Spearman(data-drive, total pairwise MI of the KC's inputs | >= 2 inputs) = +0.44
   (real), +0.55 (degree-preserving), +0.67 (ER); Spearman(KC sd, number of distinct features) =
   +0.63 / +0.59 / +0.67; KC sd by number of distinct features (1 / 2 / 3+) = 0.004 / 0.008 / 0.015
   (real), 0.006 / 0.010 / 0.018 (degree), 0.003 / 0.009 / 0.020 (ER).

Caveats: a single seed for the data, the masks (including which 72 types were drawn) and the

### Dissection (checkpoints, 12,432 eligible test rows)

**(a) Why the clamp test flips sign for the fly wiring.** Decomposing dF = F(clamped) - F(original)
into the mean-field energy terms (visible bias, KC bias, MBON bias, PN-KC coupling, KC-MBON coupling):

| term | fly wiring | degree control |
|---|---|---|
| visible bias | +5.21 | +4.32 |
| KC bias | +33.62 | +23.69 |
| MBON bias | +0.22 | -0.55 |
| PN-KC coupling | -45.43 | -39.24 |
| KC-MBON coupling | **-3.13** | **+10.42** |
| total dF | -12.63 | +9.06 |

The clamp switches on five rare features (purchase lags and purchased_within_7d; train mean 0.22)
whose 20 PN units have 2,025 KC targets in both masks. In both models the KC layer reacts the same
way (median |dh| 0.007, about 60-70 KCs flip by more than 0.1, some fully), which lowers the PN-KC
coupling energy by about 40-45 and raises the KC-bias term by 24-34; these two nearly cancel. The
sign is decided by the KC -> MBON block: in the degree control the MBON layer responds to the
flipped KCs (MBON mean activation 0.694 -> 0.728, per-unit sd median 0.28, 32 of 49 MBONs with
sd >= 0.1) and adds +10.4, i.e. the clamped configuration is penalised at the MBON level; in the fly
wiring the MBON layer is inert (mean activation 0.750 -> 0.750, sd median 0.015, 16 of 49 units
saturated, 20 with sd >= 0.1, median |dh| 0.0001) and the block contributes -3.1. The two masks have
identical MBON in-degrees (median 278, max 1,718, 4 MBONs with zero in-degree in both; the
degree-preserving control keeps column degrees) and near-identical coupling statistics (mean |w|
0.100 vs 0.099, 56 % vs 50 % positive), so the difference is the *pattern* of which KCs converge on
which MBON: in the real graph an MBON collects KCs of one lobe compartment, and half of the MBONs end
up saturated or constant under the ICONIP training, while a uniformly random KC set gives every MBON
a mixed, informative input. The marginal side is not the cause: the clamped features' PN units have
visible bias -1.05 (fly) vs -0.94 (degree), and the bias-only term is positive in both. The bias-only
correlation of dF is -0.17 (fly) vs +0.37 (degree), consistent with a coupling-dominated penalty.

**(b) The alpha CATE gap (+0.23 fly, +0.42 degree, +0.64 V0).** It is not a representation deficit.
Ridge R^2 of the true alpha from the KC belief on held-out test rows is 0.739 (fly) vs 0.741 (degree)
vs 0.739 (V0 full belief) vs 0.738 from the raw 72 features, i.e. all three beliefs carry all the
alpha information the inputs contain (alpha is a function of income and age, whose one-hots are
recovered with R^2 1.00 from either KC layer); 713 (fly) vs 819 (degree) KCs correlate with alpha at
|r| > 0.2; the income / age features reach 309 / 874 KCs in the fly mask and 306 / 878 in the
control, almost all of them with >= 2 distinct inputs. The gap is in the purchase adapter's
counterfactual contrast: the logit-scale sale1 uplift has mean +0.029, sd 0.011 (fly) and +0.032, sd
0.010 (degree) versus +0.084, sd 0.060 for V0 (the data-generating effect is alpha x 0.05, about
0.10 logit). On the 1,967-dimensional belief the adapter learns a sale1 interaction that is three
times too small and noisier day to day (within-consumer sd of the uplift / total sd 0.62 vs 0.37 for
V0), and the rank correlation with alpha suffers accordingly; averaging the uplift per consumer
raises it to +0.34 (fly), +0.62 (degree), +0.71 (V0). The fly-vs-control difference sits inside this
small-signal regime with identical uplift scale, so with one seed it cannot be separated from noise.
